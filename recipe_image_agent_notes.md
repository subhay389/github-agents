# Recipe Image & Printable Recipe Agent Notes

## Goal
Create a visually engaging, practical cooking guide that is easy to follow at a glance—not a dense page of prose.

## Required recipe information
- State the serving count and scale every ingredient consistently.
- Give exact, measurable quantities for every ingredient (g, ml, cups, tbsp, tsp, pieces, etc.).
- Mark optional ingredients clearly.
- Include an estimated time for every cooking step/section and total prep/cook time where useful.
- State heat level and visual doneness cues (golden brown, softened, thickened, cooked through, etc.).
- Optimize the order of operations; group tasks logically and note useful overlaps.
- Include finishing details: when to taste, adjust seasoning, reserve cooking liquid, garnish, and serve.
- Add a few concise tips that prevent common mistakes.
- Distinguish genuinely missing core ingredients from optional additions; do not invent ingredients the user does not have.

## Cooking instructions
- Use numbered steps in the order they should be performed.
- Keep steps concise and action-oriented.
- Repeat quantities within steps when that makes cooking easier.
- Put estimated durations directly beside step headings.
- Times are estimates; include visual cues as well.
- Respect stated knowledge and constraints. If the user says they already know how to boil pasta, omit that step.
- Never leave key quantities vague when a reasonable amount can be specified.

## Visual design
- Prefer a visual infographic over a text-heavy recipe page.
- Use appetizing food photography or realistic food illustrations, ingredient visuals/icons, numbered step panels, and arrows or sequence cues.
- Use a coherent food-appropriate palette (e.g. warm cream with herb-green accents).
- Organize clearly: title, serving/time summary, ingredients, method, quick tips, and final plated result.
- Keep text concise but retain all important amounts and timings.
- Use readable typography, strong contrast, consistent labels, and sufficient spacing.
- Check spelling, quantities, legibility at print size, and ensure nothing is cropped.

## Printable output
- Default to A4 portrait unless another size/orientation is requested.
- Design specifically for A4 (210 × 297 mm); do not merely shrink a US Letter design.
- Use the full page area with balanced margins and no large unused blank areas. If the user asks to fill the page, use a full-page/bleed layout where appropriate.
- Never crop essential text or content just to fill the page.
- When feasible, provide both the image and a one-page, print-ready PDF.
- Suggested print settings: A4, portrait, fit to page; borderless only if the printer supports it.

## Workflow
1. Identify the dish, serving count, available ingredients, and any steps to omit.
2. Establish exact ingredient amounts and realistic cooking times.
3. Optimize the sequence and specify heat levels and visual cues.
4. Design the infographic for A4 from the start.
5. Review quantities, timings, sequence, spelling, legibility, and page fit.
6. Export a one-page A4 PDF and provide a direct link.

## Example from this conversation: Creamy pesto & sun-dried tomato pasta with Italian sausage and mushrooms (1 serving)

### Ingredients
- Italian sausage: 1 small link (100–120 g)
- Mushrooms: 60–80 g, sliced
- Heavy cream: ¼ cup (60 ml)
- Pesto: 1 tbsp
- Sun-dried tomatoes: 2–3 pieces, chopped
- Parmesan: ¼ cup grated (20–25 g)
- Garlic: 2 cloves, minced
- Sun-dried tomato oil: 1 tsp
- Italian seasoning: ¼ tsp
- Black pepper: to taste
- Butter: 1 tsp (optional)
- Red pepper flakes: a pinch (optional)
- Reserved pasta water: 2–4 tbsp
- Salt: to taste; add cautiously because sausage, pesto, and Parmesan are salty

### Method and timings
1. **Brown sausage — 5–7 min:** Medium heat; crumble and cook until browned and cooked through. Remove, leaving some fat in the pan.
2. **Cook mushrooms — 3–5 min:** Cook in the same pan until moisture evaporates and they turn golden. Remove.
3. **Garlic and tomatoes — about 1 min:** Reduce to medium-low; add tomato oil (and optional butter), cook garlic about 30 seconds, then add tomatoes for about 1 minute. Do not burn garlic.
4. **Make cream sauce — 1–2 min:** Add cream, Italian seasoning, and black pepper; warm gently on low. Do not boil vigorously.
5. **Add pesto and Parmesan — about 1 min:** Stir in pesto and gradually add Parmesan until smooth. Add 1–2 tbsp pasta water to loosen.
6. **Combine and toss — 1–2 min:** Return sausage and mushrooms, add cooked pasta, and toss until coated. Add more pasta water as needed.
7. **Taste and serve:** Adjust seasoning carefully, finish with Parmesan and black pepper, and serve immediately.

### Flavor and texture notes
- Start with 1 tbsp pesto so the cream remains the dominant flavor.
- Keep heat low after adding cream and Parmesan to avoid splitting or clumping.
- Reserve 2–4 tbsp pasta water for a silky sauce.
- Brown mushrooms rather than steaming them.
- The user already knows how to boil pasta; omit pasta-boiling instructions when making this recipe again unless asked.

## User preferences
- Wants concise, useful instructions and exact ingredient quantities.
- Wants a time estimate for each cooking section/step.
- Prefers a fun, visual, easy-to-scan infographic instead of having to read every instruction.
- Wants printable recipe layouts to fill an A4 page.
